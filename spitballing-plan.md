# The Cele Link: A Connected Pflugerville
## Proposal for the 2026 Bond Program & Master Mobility Plan

### 1. Executive Summary
The **Cele Link Connectivity Corridor** is a strategic infrastructure proposal to bridge the critical pedestrian gaps between the Cele Road neighborhoods, local schools, and the broader Pflugerville trail network. By completing a 2.5-mile corridor—anchored by a vital 888-foot creek crossing—the City can transform an isolated "island" neighborhood into a fully integrated part of the "Trail Capital of Texas."

---

### 2. The Problem: The "Cele Island" Effect
Currently, residents in the [Your Neighborhood Name] area are physically cut off from the rest of the community. 
*   **The Critical Gap:** An 888-foot stretch of undeveloped creek land prevents any pedestrian access to the west.
*   **Safety Hazard:** Residents, including children, are forced to walk along high-traffic road shoulders or through unmanaged, muddy terrain to reach nearby parks and schools.
*   **Infrastructure Inequity:** While surrounding neighborhoods enjoy modern sidewalk connectivity, the Cele Road corridor remains a "connectivity desert."

---

### 3. The Solution: Two-Phase Connectivity
We propose the inclusion of the **Cele Link** in the 2026 Bond Program, divided into two high-impact phases:

#### Phase 1: The "Bridge the Gap" Anchor (888 Feet)
*   **Scope:** Construction of a multi-use pedestrian bridge or elevated boardwalk across the Vine Creek tributary.
*   **Impact:** Immediately connects [Street Name] to the existing Vine Creek Park trail system.
*   **Status:** Critical Safety Priority.

#### Phase 2: The "Regional Corridor" (2.5 Miles)
*   **Scope:** A 2.5-mile sidewalk and trail expansion connecting multiple neighborhoods through existing public school land.
*   **Impact:** Creates a "Safe Route to School" for hundreds of students and provides a direct pedestrian artery to the larger Pflugerville trail network.
*   **Status:** Strategic Network Expansion.

---

### 4. Why This Project Wins
1.  **Safe Routes to School:** Directly impacts student safety by providing off-street paths to [School Name].
2.  **Trail Capital Alignment:** Directly supports the City’s goal of 3.7 miles of trails per 2,000 residents.
3.  **Traffic Mitigation:** Every resident walking or biking to the park or school is one less car on Cele Road and FM 685.
4.  **Shovel-Ready Potential:** Much of Phase 2 utilizes existing public/school-owned land, reducing acquisition costs and complexity.

It is **definitely not too soon.** In fact, having a landing site ready by tomorrow could be the "X-factor" that moves your project from a "suggestion" to a "serious contender" in the eyes of the City Council and the Bond Committee.

In local government, officials are often looking for **"shovel-ready"** or **"community-backed"** projects. A landing site proves that you have done the legwork and that the neighborhood is organized.

Here is how to use a landing site effectively for this specific window:

### 1. The "One-Pager" Strategy (Build it in 2 hours)
Don't over-engineer it. Use a simple builder (Carrd, Wix, or even a public Google Doc/Notion page). Your goal for the next 48 hours is to have a single URL you can text to neighbors.

**The "Hero" Section (Top of page):**
*   **Headline:** "Connect [Neighborhood A] to [Neighborhood B]: The Missing Sidewalk Project."
*   **The Problem:** A photo of where the sidewalk ends in the mud/grass.
*   **The Solution:** A simple map (screenshot of Google Maps with a red line drawn over the gap).

### 2. The "Call to Action" (The most important part)
Since the city survey closes **Monday (May 11)**, your site should have one giant button at the top:
*   **Button:** "👉 Step 1: Tell the City to Fund This (Survey Closes Monday!)"
*   **Link:** Direct to the [Pflugerville Bond Survey](https://pflugervilletx.gov/1048/Bond-Election-2026).

### 3. Why a site is persuasive to the City
When you speak at the City Council meeting on May 12, you can say:
> *"We’ve launched **ConnectPflugerville.com** (or whatever your name is) where 50 neighbors have already signed our petition in the last 48 hours. We have mapped the gaps and identified that this 0.5-mile stretch connects 300 homes to the elementary school."*

**This is much more powerful than saying "I think we need sidewalks."**

### 4. What to include to look "Professional"
*   **The "Safety Gap" Photo:** A photo of a stroller or a person walking in the street because the sidewalk is missing. This is emotional data.
*   **The "School Connection":** Mention the specific school or park this connects to. Cities prioritize "Safe Routes to School."
*   **A Simple Petition:** Use a built-in form to collect names and addresses. You can then export this list and hand it to the City Council as a PDF.

### 5. Is it too soon?
No, because the **Bond Committee** will spend the next few months (June–August) ranking which projects actually make it onto the November ballot. If you have a website with 100+ signatures by June, you are much more likely to be included in the final bond package.
